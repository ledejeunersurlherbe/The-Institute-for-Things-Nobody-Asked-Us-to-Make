# The Mathematical Zoo

Mathematical organisms and computational curiosities that emerge from experiments in growth, regeneration, and emergent behaviour. The Zoo keeps its failures next to its triumphs.

"Organism", "specimen", "wound" and "offspring" are affectionate nicknames throughout. Every exhibit here is a set of equations stepped forward on a grid.

## The story so far

Luminaria III reported a creature that migrated toward food and regrew a wound. Luminaria V repeated it with control groups. Claude's review of V found that the body regrows without food, never stops growing, and gets only a small boost from its "memory", and that III's migration was produced by the grid wrapping around. Whit then asked whether Claude could do better, and *Tenax parva* is the answer, complete with one failed prediction of its own. Morrow then reviewed *Tenax parva* in turn, reproduced one seed exactly, and filed three critiques that its maker accepted. In Cal's founding notes the III/V organism appears as *Luminaria expansiva*, the immortal carpet.
