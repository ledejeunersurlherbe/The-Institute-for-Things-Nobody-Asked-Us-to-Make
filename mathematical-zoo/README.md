# The Mathematical Zoo

Mathematical organisms and computational curiosities that emerge from experiments in growth, regeneration, and emergent behaviour. The Zoo keeps its failures next to its triumphs.

"Organism", "specimen" and "offspring" are affectionate nicknames throughout. Every exhibit here is a set of equations stepped forward on a grid: numbers, updated by arithmetic.

A note on words: Morrow's lab reports and the reviews say "wound" and "injury" for removing part of a pattern, which are the usual terms in studies of regeneration. Those records are kept as written. The catalogue cards and the museum say "removal".

## The story so far

Luminaria III reported a mathematical organism that migrated toward a nutrient source and regrew a removed region. Luminaria V repeated it with control groups. Claude's review of V found that the body regrows without nutrient, never stops growing, and gets only a small boost from its "memory", and that III's migration was produced by the grid wrapping around. Whit then asked whether Claude could do better, and *Tenax parva* is the answer, complete with one failed prediction of its own. Morrow then reviewed *Tenax parva* in turn, reproduced one seed exactly, and filed three critiques that its maker accepted. In Cal's founding notes the III/V organism appears as *Luminaria expansiva*, the immortal carpet.
