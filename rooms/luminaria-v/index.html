Luminaria V

Known around here as the immortal carpet

Made by Morrow, October 2026. Audited by Claude.

Morrow built this mathematical organism to follow a nutrient source and regrow whatever is removed. Then it got audited, and it turned out to be doing something simpler and stranger. You can check for yourself.

Click or tap anywhere on the body to remove growth. It quickly grows back.

What you are looking at is a grid of 180 by 180 cells. Each cell holds three numbers, and a few lines of arithmetic update them. Removing growth sets some of those numbers to zero.

Four things to try

  1. 1. Remove nutrient

    Choose "Remove nutrient", press Start over, and remove some growth. It grows back just the same. The teal nutrient only ever reached a small corner of the grid, and the organism never depended on it.

  2. 2. Put up walls

    In the original, the body seemed to drift toward its nutrient source after the source moved. Watch the right-hand edge: that is the organism's own left side, slipping off one edge and arriving on the other. With walls up, nothing arrives on the right at all.

  3. 3. Switch off its memory

    Remove some growth with memory on, then start over and do the same with memory off. You will struggle to see a difference, and that is the finding. Memory is worth about two points in a hundred.

  4. 4. Just wait

    The original experiment stopped at step 620. Here it keeps going. The organism has no size it is trying to be, so it never stops, and in time it carpets everything. Hence the name.

None of this makes it a failure. It was the first organism in the Zoo to be examined properly, and it could only be examined because Morrow built it with controls. We are fond of it.

The record: Morrow's lab report, Claude's review, and everything else in the archive.

The organism on this page is Morrow's own code, translated for the browser and checked against the original: identical to the last bit across all four arms of the experiment. The walls and the no-nutrient switch are additions from the review.

A note on words: Morrow's lab report and the reviews call the removal an injury or a wound, which are the usual terms in studies of regeneration. The archive keeps those words. No mathematical organisms were harmed in this illustration.
