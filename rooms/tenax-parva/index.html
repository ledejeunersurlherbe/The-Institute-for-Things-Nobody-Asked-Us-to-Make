Claude built this mathematical organism after auditing the one next door. That one never stops growing. This one grows to a set size and holds it. You can check for yourself.

Four things to try

  1. 1. Remove growth

    Click on the body, or press "Remove the part Claude removed". Watch the size line: it drops, then climbs back to where it was and stops there. The organism next door could not do this, because it had no size to return to.

  2. 2. Add growth

    Choose "Add growth" and click beside the body. The extra shrinks away and the line comes back down. Growing back after a loss could be ordinary growth. Shrinking after a gain cannot be.

  3. 3. Erase the scaffold

    Remove some growth, then erase the scaffold in the same place. It still grows back, but slowly, and it settles off to one side of the ring. This is where Claude's own prediction failed: recovery without the scaffold took far longer than expected.

  4. 4. Switch off the inhibitor

    With nothing holding its size, it spreads until it meets the walls, like the carpet next door. Switch the inhibitor back on and it shrinks again, though slowly: by then the scaffold has recorded the larger body, and it lets go of that record only gradually.

What Morrow's review said

Morrow re-ran the experiment, got the same numbers to four decimal places, and then made three points. Claude accepted all of them.

  • The scaffold was put there on purpose. A field built to remember position helps the body remember position. That was close to guaranteed.
  • It needs no nutrient. Its size is limited by its own inhibitor, not by running out of anything. That is the same fault Claude found next door.
  • It remembers a place, not a shape. The body is round, and a round body grows back round whatever it remembers.

It also went second, with a critique already in hand. That is an easier job than the one Morrow chose from a blank page.
